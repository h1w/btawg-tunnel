# Extended handoff: fresh-flight feedback, carrier-only reopen, and existing TCP fallback

**Scope.** Real Linux client/hub, eight continuous CUBIC downloads through kernel TUN, same CPU binding and MTU as the [delivery-probe study](../utp-delivery-probes/summary.md). Pacing environment 50 Mbit/s at both endpoints; TCP carrier ignores it. No product binaries, defaults or managed settings were changed. Two independent loss seeds, one run per cell; scheduling changes packet loss positions. Compare rates *within* the stated window. `late_mbps` is ten seconds starting ~25 s after the LAN→WAN step, not total transfer speed. `strong` first runs on that WAN for 60 s, then adds a shared 2 Mbit/s / 8 kB physical FIFO and one independent direct CUBIC for 24 s. All traffic has 200 ms RTT; only the test carrier has independent 1% loss per direction. The cross-flow has no artificial loss but can lose packets in the shared FIFO. `overlap_mbps` and p95 cover +1…+22 s after the cut; CUBIC rate is its iperf receiver result. `shared` is a separate 10 Mbit/s FIFO case with both flows impaired.

**A cut frontier, not a new default.** `credit` is the previous fast delivery-confirmed lab controller. `fresh` differs by remembering the exclusive byte offset of the most recently first-transmitted µTP original. After lowering its experimental credit it disallows further credit cuts and regrowth until an advancing cumulative ACK covers at least one byte beyond that frontier. It does not suppress ordinary µTP congestion backoff or retransmissions. Unit tests reproduced unchanged-edge compounding (48,000 → 2,026 B in the old lab controller) and checked both the inclusive old boundary and a new post-cut byte. Tests of both ordered ACK accounting and the new regression passed under the race detector. The receiver-edge invariant was also checked against every consecutive hub retreat in the recorded trials.

The guard did block stale feedback. In the healthy-neighbor profile the final observed suppressed-event counters were 414 and 361; the hub made 28/52 new cuts after contraction versus 72/63 in the previous controller. But it didn't consistently eliminate application stalls: 1/9 consecutive zero seconds after the cut versus 2/1 in fresh `credit` comparisons. The 29-second result from the prior study was not reproduced in these two comparator runs; don't attribute a 29→9 improvement to the guard. During competition `fresh` delivered 0.509/0.202 Mbit/s instead of the previous controller's 0.136/0.160, still below the unmodified µTP's 0.883/0.883. The healthy CUBIC got 1.13/1.34 Mbit/s vs 0.476/0.390 with unmodified µTP. This is an improvement in one mechanism, not a qualified cure for self-starvation.

**Carrier replacement without closing the TUN.** `reopen` uses an unchanged baseline hub and unchanged baseline µTP controller on the client. A diagnostic client hook closes only the current carrier once, four seconds after the imposed route switch; it re-enters normal discovery/handshake/attach without recreating the AWG device, TUN, listening workload or the eight TCP sockets. This is a known-time diagnostic intervention, not automatic route detection and **not a signal understood by the released client**. The two WAN-only trials observed two attachments, one forced close and receiver progress on all eight original sockets. First positive per-socket samples after replacement varied from about 3 to 12 s across these runs. Late receiver goodput was 17.30/21.64 Mbit/s, versus 0.534/1.281 on an uninterrupted unmodified µTP connection. This shows that a full client restart or moving application sessions is not inherently required to reset transport state. It does not prove that every in-flight connection survives every handoff or that AWG FEC caused the gain (hub coded-pieces counter stayed zero).

**Not a safe automatic reconnect rule.** At the later real 2 Mbit/s bottleneck `reopen` gave CUBIC only 0.347/0.000 Mbit/s vs 0.476/0.390 on unchanged µTP (seeds 47011/47032). Physical FIFO drops over the overlap were 14,389/16,099. The cap had been configured at 50 Mbit/s. Reopening on a real underlay change is promising for a stable capacious WAN, but too aggressive on a subsequent capacity collapse. No test signal or periodic reconnect should be deployed. Existing cold-connection pacing results are in the [residual study](../extended-utp-residual/summary.md).

**Deployable control.** `outertcp` uses the actual baseline binaries with `socks5-awg-ext-tcp`, not a patched congestion algorithm. The short WAN profile preserved the same established flows as the uninterrupted µTP reference. In the healthy-neighbor case the dedicated lossy class matches the carrier's TCP port 6881 in each direction, not TCP traffic in general; the direct iperf port and ICMP remain delay-only. The product's default managed fixed SOCKS ports are 1081 for AWG Extended/TCP and 1080 for AWG Extended/µTP. Selecting another fixed port affects new SOCKS flows, not live migrations.

**Practical outcome.** With stock Extended/TCP after the 200 ms / 1% WAN step, useful download goodput across the whole 38-second post-change window was **32.72 / 18.86 Mbit/s** and the longest blank was 0 / 1 full second. The late 10-second windows were 23.16 / 23.79. At a physical bottleneck the sender alone cannot promise a fair share: even this TCP mode missed the 90% CUBIC gate once with a single shared FIFO.

**Controllable egress.** Kept the 2 Mbit/s shaper after the 60-second ramp but replaced the single 8 kB FIFO with two work-conserving DRR classes (equal 1514-byte quantum; 4 kB FIFO in each). Carrier downloads from port 6881 map to one class and other traffic to the other. The queue acts before propagation/random loss. Both class byte/packet counters were checked, and no host/production network settings were touched. In two Extended/TCP trials useful tunnel / CUBIC Mbit/s changed from **0.549 / 0.390 → 0.520 / 0.737** and **0.571 / 0.477 → 0.552 / 0.780**; physical loaded p95 changed from 224/225 to 213/212 ms. Equal scheduling of wire bytes is not equal application goodput nor zero loss. This is actionable only when the limiting egress is under your control (or you can shape just below that downstream rate); port, direction, burst and capacity are fixture parameters, not safe universal host defaults. `iso-utp` uses the same scheduler but still depends on the diagnostic reopen and suffers many carrier-class drops; not a proposed default.
