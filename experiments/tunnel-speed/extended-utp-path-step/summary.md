# Extended/µTP: long application stalls on a live propagation step

Same local real-TUN client/hub workload as the [ordered-carrier comparison](../awg-fast-ordered-four/summary.md): eight CUBIC downloads already running for 5 s, then a 200 ms RTT, 1% random loss per direction, no rate limiter. Two loss seeds, two non-instrumented runs per seed and build (the two originally reported runs plus two fresh baselines). The 38 complete one-second receiver intervals exclude the one straddling the switch. `longest_zero_s` is consecutive zero-progress intervals, not the sum `zero_s`. `late_mbps` covers ten seconds beginning at the first sample ≥24 s after the change. One zero-progress second is not a disconnect.

| Non-instrumented metric across four runs | Before: median [range] | Final: median [range] |
|---|---:|---:|
| Longest sequence of zero seconds | 31.5 [3–34] | 5 [3–8] |
| Total zero seconds out of 38 | 34 [14–35] | 12 [4–15] |
| Receiver Mbit/s in late window | 0 [0–0.0314] | 0.702 [0.347–1.050] |
| Receiver Mbit/s across post-change window | 1.482 [1.294–3.180] | 2.103 [1.765–2.984] |

**Mechanism.** In the original reported runs there were 32 and 34 consecutive zero seconds; the client's overlay connection did not close during either window. A new stock state trace (seed 47032) contained a 30-second plateau. At +2 s after the change the receiver held ~6.1 MB of out-of-order µTP bytes while the sender recovered gaps; at +8 s the reorder buffer had emptied. Sender sequence and cumulative ACK continued to advance, peer advertised 24 MiB of credit, but hub cwnd stayed at 1,382 bytes (one segment). Its measured one-way excess was ~100.15 ms vs a 100 ms LEDBAT target, with the reverse excess ~100.17 ms. The controller compares to a pre-change LAN minimum (13 one-minute base slots); after a loss/timeout shrinks the flight there is no positive window gain from those ACKs. Unlike the older SACK/RTO bug this is not one µTP sequence number stuck for the whole measurement. With a single tiny outer flight, frame and inner TCP ordering matter; the presence of UDP or even µTP ACK progress does not mean that all eight inner streams receive new contiguous bytes. A separate TUN-header-only replay showed early out-of-order inner TCP data and, later, progress concentrated in one flow. It was not the same random trajectory as either original freeze.

**Interventions.** `trace`: old algorithm + sampled connection state (500 ms); `reset`: same trace, with one lab-only 99 ms forward-base shift at near-minimum cwnd on the known WAN. In two seeds median late cwnd went from 1,382 B to ~19–20 kB, without changing timers or FEC. This alone did not consistently increase useful late goodput on both seeds. `small`: unrelated experiment shrinking the AWG peer sender buffer to four batches; `both`: small + reset. Neither change to the AWG buffer nor hard-coded known delay is in production. `fix` / `fixtrace`: first heuristic, rejected after it rebased on a truly full duplex FIFO while far more bytes were in flight than the current cwnd allowed. `final` / `finaltrace`: only the vendor µTP congestion policy changes; original and intermediate builds are not pooled with it.

**Final rule.** Only at a nearly collapsed window, with flight drained to the new limit, corroborating smoothed RTT and fresh ACK timing, stable comparable delay increases in both directions for at least two seconds can retire half of the common delay increment from the forward baseline. Sporadic reports and one-way queue inflation do not qualify. No floor boost, new packets, change to RTT/loss timers, or bypass of peer credit. The shared pure-Go µTP controller is changed; the end-to-end measurements here are for Extended/AWG download, not every consumer of µTP.

**Controls.** Clean local goodput in the complete post-switch window: 331.87 → 333.69 Mbit/s, zero zero-progress seconds, one trial per arm. With one 55 Mbit/s UDP competitor on a 100 Mbit/s FIFO and no artificial delay the competitor received 54.7 → 54.9 Mbit/s; tunnel late goodput was 35.74 → 35.88 Mbit/s. With both FIFOs saturated by 103 Mbit/s of UDP in each direction, underlay p95 was 240 ms; the final instrumented hub and client made zero reclassifications in that control. This is not a proof of fairness on all paths. Stable bidirectional queues and a genuine route-length increase are not always distinguishable from delay feedback. Real bottleneck congestion and random outer loss can still produce long stalls or low transfer rates.
