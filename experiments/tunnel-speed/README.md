# WG/AWG hub performance handoff

## Start here

**Public evidence:** the [five-mode qualification](awg-auto-five/summary.md) includes a replayable, sanitized raw archive. Earlier reports and measurement tables are public; references in them to other raw capture archives refer to local lab files, not repository downloads.

- **Current six-mode delay-only matrix:** [`zero-loss-delay/summary.md`](zero-loss-delay/summary.md). Native WG/AWG and all four AWG BitTorrent carriers without TLS, real TUN, 0% injected loss, no rate cap, 0 and 100 ms one way, TCP CUBIC download/upload with 1 and 8 streams. Includes actual queue-drop deltas and two cold-tunnel repeats of stalled cells.
- **Hub queue overflow:** [`hub-queue-overflow/summary.md`](hub-queue-overflow/summary.md). Why a no-loss underlay can still see hub drops, native-only retry A/B at 0 and +200 ms RTT, and rejected queue/batch alternatives.
- **AWG carrier queue overflow:** [`carrier-queue-overflow/summary.md`](carrier-queue-overflow/summary.md). Four carrier modes at 0 and +200 ms RTT: queue batching, Request/piece allocation reuse, TCP backpressure and TUN burst-size controls. No production default adopted.
- **Extended/µTP stage profile:** [`extended-utp-stages/summary.md`](extended-utp-stages/summary.md). Separates TUN/AWG queue wait, encryption, protected record writes, µTP congestion-window waits and UDP submission CPU cost at 0 and +200 ms RTT.
- **Extended/µTP ready-only batching:** [`extended-utp-ready-udp/summary.md`](extended-utp-ready-udp/summary.md). No timer or queue growth; ~70% fewer UDP submissions per MiB and lower loaded RTT, but more local multi-flow drops and no WAN throughput gain. Lab only.
- **Extended/µTP split batching:** [`extended-utp-split-batching/summary.md`](extended-utp-split-batching/summary.md). Four-arm 2×2: ready AWG collection improves WAN goodput; frame batching alone is nearly inert, but in combination amplifies local multi-flow drops and gives up WAN speed versus ready AWG collection alone.
- **Extended/µTP local overflow:** [`extended-utp-local-overflow/summary.md`](extended-utp-local-overflow/summary.md). Matched 280/480 Mbit/s offered load, TUN-read queue-depth buckets, and same-binary GSO control separate burst effects from saturation.
- **Extended/µTP ready AWG at 0% and 1%:** [`extended-utp-awg-ready-loss/summary.md`](extended-utp-awg-ready-loss/summary.md). AWG-only collection improved clean throughput, but failed the lossy completion gate, including fresh eight-stream controls. Lab only.
- **Extended/µTP ready AWG pairs:** [`extended-utp-awg-ready-pair/summary.md`](extended-utp-awg-ready-pair/summary.md). Fresh-tunnel A/B at 0% and 1% with at most two ready packets. Clean local download +23–29%; both arms completed 3/8 at 1%. Lab only.
- **Extended/µTP first stall:** [`extended-utp-first-stall/summary.md`](extended-utp-first-stall/summary.md). Two-sided capture at 1%: a lost DATA packet and lost fast retransmit leave a gap; SACKs for later packets repeatedly postpone its RTO.
- **Extended/µTP RTO fix:** [`extended-utp-rto-fix/summary.md`](extended-utp-rto-fix/summary.md). Later SACKs no longer postpone recovery of the oldest unacknowledged packet. At 1%, local completion 1/4 → 4/4; +200 ms eight-stream timeouts remain.
- **Extended/µTP WAN timeout trace:** [`extended-utp-wan-stall/summary.md`](extended-utp-wan-stall/summary.md). At 1%, +200 ms and eight streams the carrier still delivered traffic; 128 KiB iperf3 reads hid progress, and the TCP control/result exchange timed out even with 4 KiB reads.
- **Extended/µTP WAN window accounting:** [`extended-utp-wan-cwnd/summary.md`](extended-utp-wan-cwnd/summary.md). TCP receiver counters at +200 ms and eight streams: 142.7–236.3 Mbit/s clean, 0.580–0.582 at 1%. All observed lossy window cuts came from fast retransmit, not delay or RTO.
- **Extended/µTP RTT-aware loss backoff:** [`extended-utp-rtt-backoff/summary.md`](extended-utp-rtt-backoff/summary.md). One fast-loss cut per RTT fixes repeated backoff, not the 1% throughput collapse. Existing opt-in pacing reached 12–13 Mbit/s but overloaded the 10 Mbit/s queue at the tested cap.
- **Extended/µTP adaptive pacing:** [`extended-utp-adaptive-pacing/summary.md`](extended-utp-adaptive-pacing/summary.md). Unlocked waits improved 1% goodput to 2.26 Mbit/s with no 10 Mbit/s qdisc drops, but collapsed clean goodput. Both prototypes rejected as defaults.
- **Extended/µTP bounded pacing probes:** [`extended-utp-paced-probe/summary.md`](extended-utp-paced-probe/summary.md). 3.23–3.51× over the previous adaptive pacer on unshaped paths; 1,597–1,657 excess qdisc drops on the 10 Mbit/s test. Lab only.
- **Extended/µTP 10 Mbit/s queue trace:** [`extended-utp-queue-trace/summary.md`](extended-utp-queue-trace/summary.md). New DATA filled the 160-packet queue before the first retry. Later retries were only 11–13% of ingress IP bytes but 78–79% of matched drops.
- **Extended/µTP shared DATA budget:** [`extended-utp-shared-budget/summary.md`](extended-utp-shared-budget/summary.md). ACK bypass retained; 10 Mbit/s goodput +13–14%, retry drops −88–90%, total drops +31–44%. Lab only.
- **Extended/µTP quiet WAN backoff:** [`extended-utp-quiet-loss-cut/summary.md`](extended-utp-quiet-loss-cut/summary.md). A gentler cut raised 1% WAN goodput 2.4–2.6×, but increased overflow from 216 to 386 drops on the 10 Mbit/s control. Lab only.
- **Extended/µTP window policy vs TCP CUBIC:** [`utp-window-fairness/summary.md`](utp-window-fairness/summary.md). Guarded 7/8 backoff and a 5 ms WAN delay target gained 2.27–3.15× at 1% loss alone, but did not speed up µTP with a competing CUBIC flow and failed one lossy coexistence gate. Lab only.
- **Hub/client queue and µTP recovery audit:** [`queue-recovery-matrix/summary.md`](queue-recovery-matrix/summary.md). Ten-mode A/B separates endpoint drops from transit loss. Fixed TLS TUN recovery, stale primary routing, IPv4 fragment delivery and admission, and µTP ACK, receiver-credit and retransmit timing bugs. Queue saturation still occurs on clean links; at 1% +200 ms µTP can stay below 1 Mbit/s with no endpoint queue drops. Lab parity improved transparent native WG/AWG but was not adopted.
- **AWG TCP outer socket controls:** [`carrier-tcp-buffer-control/summary.md`](carrier-tcp-buffer-control/summary.md). Receive autotune is not a common win at 0/1% and 0/+200 ms; fixed buffers did not limit the measured lossy WAN sender. Same-binary TCP offload control changes `netem` work-item granularity without improving goodput. Lab only.
- **Extended/µTP ready pairs after recovery fixes:** [`extended-utp-ready-recovery/summary.md`](extended-utp-ready-recovery/summary.md). All 16 intervals completed; clean local download +26%, clean WAN +22% with 69% more hub drops, lossy WAN still below 1 Mbit/s. Lab only.
- **µTP outbound DATA allocation:** [`utp-outbound-allocation/summary.md`](utp-outbound-allocation/summary.md). One allocation instead of two cut loopback allocation count 17.8%, but increased allocated bytes 2.6%; equal-load UDP saw no repeatable gain and clean WAN upload regressed in the single pair. Lab only.
- **µTP short-write receive credit:** [`utp-short-write-credit/summary.md`](utp-short-write-credit/summary.md). Four-byte write no longer waits for a full MTU of credit. Recorded 32 managed TUN A/B attempts; lossy WAN remains below 1 Mbit/s.
- **Native Linux SOCKS upload pressure gate:** [`native-upload-credit/summary.md`](native-upload-credit/summary.md). At +200 ms on the 300 Mbit/s lab link, 0% WG/AWG rose from 51 to 133–134 Mbit/s; 1% rose from 25–27 to 59–69 Mbit/s without additional transit overflow. The gate stayed off on LAN and the 10 Mbit/s control. Not a transparent TUN or µTP change.
- **Current transparent TUN and µTP timer check:** [`transparent-current/summary.md`](transparent-current/summary.md). Native WG/AWG stays below 10 Mbit/s at 1% +200 ms without endpoint drops in this run. The µTP timer fix respects peer credit but does not accelerate WAN; a stricter loss-cut guard reached 1.37–1.54 Mbit/s and remains lab-only.
- **Native queue synchronization at 800 Mbit/s offered:** [`queue-sync-profile/summary.md`](queue-sync-profile/summary.md). Enqueue CPU was under 2% at either endpoint; replacing route wall-clock reads reduced clock samples but not total CPU or goodput across modes. Lab only.
- **Transparent TUN sender CC and fixed load:** [`transparent-cc-load/summary.md`](transparent-cc-load/summary.md). At 1% +200 ms BBR on application senders raised native WG/AWG from 9–10 to 339–418 Mbit/s; µTP stayed below 1 Mbit/s. Matched 1 Gbit/s UDP shows strong dependence on hub CPU availability, not a queue-size fix.
- **µTP sparse SACK recovery:** [`utp-sparse-recovery/summary.md`](utp-sparse-recovery/summary.md). Earlier repair of the oldest gap retained ordinary backoff, but both longer 1% +200 ms trials stayed near 0.6 Mbit/s. Lab only.
- **µTP directional loss and ACK feedback:** [`utp-directional-feedback/summary.md`](utp-directional-feedback/summary.md). Forward-only 1% reproduced the full +200 ms collapse; reverse-only loss was less severe. Faster ACKs did not recover goodput and made clean/narrow controls worse. Lab only.
- **µTP MTU probe and first-flight RTO:** [`utp-mtu-probe/summary.md`](utp-mtu-probe/summary.md). MTU discovery records the packet sent and accepts selective confirmation; the first RTO uses the outgoing packet timestamp. The lossy WAN throughput ceiling remains.
- **Matched clean A/B:** [`clean-native-ab/summary.md`](clean-native-ab/summary.md). Original «ДО» binaries (verified against archived SHA-256) vs the final source build, with byte-identical clients. Local Linux TUN, no balancer or rate limit, 0% induced loss, zero added delay, Linux offload defaults, MTU 1280, CUBIC, 1 and 8 streams, two runs per build in ABBA order. All 32 trials valid. The eight-stream download medians were WG **635 → 1517 Mbit/s (2.39×)** and AWG **636 → 1802 Mbit/s (2.83×)**, with loaded p95 RTT **20.65 → 4.68 ms** and **20.05 → 4.29 ms**, respectively. Upload did not show a common gain: WG −15%, AWG +5% in the eight-stream series.
- **Original lossy matrix:** [`before/summary.txt`](before/summary.txt), 10 modes, 1% independent loss per direction. **Same-method replay:** [`after/comparison.md`](after/comparison.md), five AWG modes plus native WG; four TLS modes skipped. Delays 0/60/100 ms one way; offloads disabled on the lab veth interfaces for per-datagram loss. No general speedup confirmed in this profile. Do not compare a clean result directly with this matrix to estimate the effect of code changes.

## Consolidated defaults

Keep both changesets together: the native WG/AWG hub fast path for packet forwarding, and the automatic Linux native SOCKS TCP path for lossy WAN. They operate at different layers. No user-facing WAN switch or transport selector was added.

- **In the working tree:** batched native TUN reads and indexed reverse-flow LRU; SOCKS TCP half-close through direct and routed sessions; per-transport `engine_queue_drops` in authenticated `/api/traffic`; automatic BBR for Linux real-TUN native SOCKS data sockets; an authorized, overlay-only hub TCP relay; bounded WAN relay send-buffer growth and pacing based on acknowledged delivery and queue delay. See the [operator documentation](../../btawg/README.md#run-the-server-vps).
- **Compatibility:** update the hub and Linux client to get the complete SOCKS path. A refused relay connection on an older hub uses the previous NAT path; a relay denial does not. The SOCKS acceleration does not change transparent TUN forwarding, UDP, other transports, the native wire format, MTU, system TCP defaults or qdiscs. The Windows client does not take this Linux socket path.
- **Keep in the lab:** interleaved FEC, UDP duplication, fixed or experimental pacing, larger global buffers, bottleneck AQM variants and HTTP Range scheduling. Interleaved FEC improved the same-file 1% trial from **79 to 126 Mbit/s** with zero overflow, but the contention/latency tradeoff is not suitable for unconditional use. Automatic negotiated recovery is not part of this changeset.

Read the numbers by profile: the original [clean 0% WG/AWG A/B](clean-native-ab/summary.md) measures forwarding, not the new SOCKS relay. The [200 ms SOCKS A/B](wan-auto-pacing/summary.md) measures an 8 MiB-accounted WAN socket and automatic pacing: **46.7 → 54.2 Mbit/s** at 1%, **42.1 → 48.9 Mbit/s** with competing UDP, both at **201 ms p95**. The later [256 MiB native control](wan-fec-flight/summary.md) measured **79.4 Mbit/s** with the integrated path. These are distinct workloads, not successive speedups to multiply.

Final combined-tree verification: race tests for server, client, SOCKS, TUN setup, route proxy, panel and e2e passed; Linux hub/client built and Windows x64 client cross-built. On isolated real TUN at +200 ms and 1% loss, both WG and AWG completed separate 8 MiB downloads and 4 MiB uploads with SHA-256 verification and zero hub engine queue drops. Direct WAN access to the overlay relay was refused. The short transfers are startup-heavy compatibility checks, not bulk speed measurements.

## Production changes

- [`router.go`](../../btawg/internal/server/router.go): native WG/AWG read up to 16 ready packets without waiting to fill the batch; the shared router continues after recoverable TUN segmentation overflow; pooled packet pointers avoid boxing slice headers; route lookup takes one timestamp per read batch. Route ownership and ACL are still checked per packet.
- [`flow_routes.go`](../../btawg/internal/server/flow_routes.go): reverse-flow LRU updates in O(1), retaining expiry since the last authenticated pin, eviction order, and capacity. With 8192 seeded table entries, WG download rose from 88 to 1295 Mbit/s and AWG from 77 to 1257 Mbit/s in the paired lab test. This is not a test of 8192 simultaneous downloads.
- Regression tests: [`router_flow_test.go`](../../btawg/internal/server/router_flow_test.go), [`flow_routes_test.go`](../../btawg/internal/server/flow_routes_test.go). Server, client, and e2e suites were run during the work; the final LRU pass ran `go test -race ./internal/server`.
