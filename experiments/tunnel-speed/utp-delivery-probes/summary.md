# Extended/µTP: delivery-confirmed window probes versus competing TCP

Real local Linux TUN, production hub and client executables with a lab-only pure-Go µTP overlay. Baseline is the **same prior path-step implementation**, with `BTAWG_UTP_PACE_MBPS=50` at both endpoints in every comparison. No changes to production defaults, framing, queues or AWG parity. Compare with the [earlier configured-BDP experiment](../extended-utp-residual/summary.md); do not mix its goodput or TCP references into the current A/B.

**Decision: lab only.** 39 valid real-tunnel trials and four independent TCP-only calibrations. The faster arm (`credit`) improved late solo WAN delivery on all four pairs: median **1.351 → 5.299 Mbit/s** (3.92×), whole post-switch window **1.306 → 3.837 Mbit/s** (2.94×). It passed the CUBIC/RTT gate in **10/11** competing-flow pairs, not all 11. The missed pair (shared 10 Mbit/s, seed 47032, repetition 2) retained 0.947/1.200 Mbit/s = **78.9%** of CUBIC, with p95 3 ms lower and zero overlap FIFO drops in either arm; the hub never began probing in that candidate trial. This fails the declared gate, but does not attribute the CUBIC difference to window growth.

|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 Mbit/s shared from switch | 3/4 | 78.9–144.2% | −3…+2 ms |

The clean control did not enter probe mode. On a genuinely congested path the problem was frequently **over-yielding**, not aggressive growth. In the healthy-neighbor profile the sender was delivering 10.04/13.79 Mbit/s before the 2 Mbit/s cap, then 0.246/0.000 Mbit/s during overlap. CUBIC received 1.60/1.43 Mbit/s versus 0.433/0.347 with baseline µTP; overlap FIFO drops fell from 152/155 to 34/59. In the second run the first retreat followed the cap by 0.38 s, with 45 retreats recorded after the cap; all 28 full post-cut receiver seconds were blank. In the lossy-neighbor late-cut test one run had a 22-second zero streak. These outcomes are not an acceptable general handoff policy even if protecting TCP alone is the goal. Next discriminator: do not compound multiplicative credit cuts on already outstanding pre-cut data; require a fresh post-cut flight before another cut, then repeat the same healthy-neighbor and random-loss gates. No such change was assumed safe here.

## What the sender actually measures

A private ring tracks the cumulative stream byte offset of each µTP DATA sequence. A valid advancing cumulative ACK supplies the ordered byte count even if some packets have already been retired via SACK. Retries and SACK-only advances do not add delivery. This is ordered **outer transport** delivery, not TCP application goodput: the latter is measured independently from TCP_INFO at the remote endpoint. The experiment measures a rate over elapsed time, not an increase for each individual ACK. A deterministic wire fixture covers an out-of-order SACK, subsequent cumulative ACK across 16-bit wrap, and a duplicate ACK. Controller tests cover flat delivery, improved delivery and rollback on queue pressure, including during probe cooldown.

After a confirmed symmetric path step, the paced sender seeds its budget from the current collapsed window, not from pacing limit × RTT. It temporarily increases that budget, ignores one settling interval, and retains the increase only when newly cumulatively delivered bytes/s are at least 8% higher than before the probe. Otherwise it rolls back and waits. Queue inflation or RTO retracts credit. The configured cap is still respected. No new wire signal, SACK format or receiver grant. Neither arm is a deployed implementation. The per-connection test ring and decision logs are lab instrumentation, not a proposed permanent allocation/logging policy.

- `probe`, conservative: 2 RTT sampling intervals (at least 400 ms); trial increase ≤25% with a 1 MSS minimum and a 4 MSS cap; 8 ms forward-delay veto. Slow ramp from as little as 1,382 bytes made it slower than baseline in both late WAN windows.
- `credit`, faster: intervals ≥1.25 RTT and ≥250 ms; increase 50% with a 2 MSS minimum and a 16 MSS cap; 5 ms forward-delay veto. Growth toward the tested budget is ACK-clocked. On both variants every positive test must be followed by a fresh measured reference; no immediate jump to a configured WAN-sized flight. A rebound after an unusually low reference can still look like improvement. This is a limitation of using only our own delivery signal.

## Acceptance and scenarios

The two preannounced gates are **per paired run**: CUBIC receiver goodput at least 90% of the reference (unmodified µTP competing under the same profile), and loaded physical p95 RTT at most 10 ms higher. Measuring TCP alone is a separate reference. The verdict does not pool away failed seeds or repeats. Seed fixes the impairment configuration, not which packet is lost when the traffic mix changes.

Eight CUBIC downloads in one selected Extended/AWG µTP tunnel start on LAN; five seconds later the WAN has 100 ms each way and 1% independent random packet loss per direction. `late_mbps` is receiver goodput over seconds ~25–35 after the switch, **not a long-transfer peak**. The 44 s runs have 38 whole post-switch seconds. `shared` adds a separate 10 Mbit/s 32 kB downstream FIFO and a single direct CUBIC competing flow at the switch; `capacity` applies these 18 s later. This earlier contraction did not always constrain an already fast sender. `late` therefore runs for 94 s and cuts the downstream rate to **2 Mbit/s, 8 kB FIFO, after 60 s of WAN ramp**, with one CUBIC flow active for 24 s. `strong` uses that same ramp and bottleneck, with netem random loss confined to outer UDP; the TCP competitor retains the same propagation delay but not the independent drops. Both still share the same physical FIFO. Congestion is not simulated by adding delay to the FIFO: propagation and the FIFO are independent.

The whole iperf receiver measurement gives competitor throughput. `overlap_mbps`, `overlap_p95_ms` and FIFO drop deltas are measured during competition (shared: +4…+32 s; capacity: +1…+15 s after the cut; late/strong: +1…+22 s after the cut). `pre_capacity_mbps` uses the last eight seconds before the cut, excluding the boundary. Compare `pre_capacity_mbps` to the new capacity to see whether a real overload was exercised. The drop counter here counts excess real FIFO drops, **not** injected random netem losses. `keep` and `flat` count the hub's delivery decisions; if no hub trial was attempted in a run, coexistence in that run is not evidence that an active upward probe is safe.
